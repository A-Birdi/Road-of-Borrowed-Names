# Story bible — The Road of Borrowed Names

Tone: warm, restrained humour, domestic detail, wonder, loneliness, occasional
unease. Small human problems first; implications grow. Not every scene is
cryptic. People talk about cold tea, bad boots, borrowed tools.

## World
- **The lantern roads** (灯の道 ひのみち): old roads marked by lantern posts.
  Each lantern shade carries the written name of the next place and the
  keeper's promise that the road leads there. *Fiction:* while the names are
  kept, the roads hold together. When a name fades, the way forgets itself —
  bridges stop reaching, paths loop, people forget who they were waiting for.
- **Lantern keepers** (灯守 ひもり — a fictional title; label it as such in
  notes) rewrite and tend the lantern names.
- **Inkweaving** (fiction): writing with clear intent so that an ordinary
  word's ordinary meaning takes form for a moment (water cools, light
  reveals). Language facts stay real; only the effect is magic. Never claim
  that a kana or kanji "means" something it doesn't.
- **The Still Archive** (静寂の書庫 しじまのしょこ): built generations ago in
  the mountains above Lanternfall to keep copies of every name and promise so
  the region could recover from floods and fires.
- **The Hush** (静寂 しじま): the quiet force now lifting names, disagreements
  and ambiguities out of the world. It makes things orderly, peaceful and
  increasingly empty.

## Antagonist — Kasane (カサネ), Keeper of the Still Archive
Thirty years ago Lanternfall flooded. Two towns had argued over the sluice
gates for weeks; the last message between them was a vague promise — 「必要なら
開ける」 ("we'll open it if it's needed") — that each side read differently.
Kasane's younger brother **Tōya** drowned. Their last conversation was an
argument Kasane never finished.
Kasane began by taking painful memories from grieving people *who asked* (a
mercy). Then disagreements (because they caused the flood). Then ambiguities
(because a vague promise killed Tōya). Now the Hush takes without asking.
Kasane is sincere, tired, articulate and wrong; understandable, not absolved.
They decided for everyone.

## Player
A wayfarer arriving at Reedwake the morning after a storm, carrying a letter of
introduction for Keeper Tsuru (flavour varies by background). They can still
read the old lantern letters and inkweave — Tsuru notices when a lantern name
the player rewrites *holds*. Referred to by name; pronouns from creation.

## Companions (exactly one travels with the player)
Adults, not one-note. Each: distinct rhythm, a Reedwake relationship, a moment
that contradicts first impressions, personal quest, banter, reactions to the
big decisions, coordinated technique, ending variation.
- **Nao** (ナオ), courier. Ochre scarf, overstuffed satchel, checks exits while
  apparently listening. Wry, perceptive, impatient with ceremony. Casual
  speech (だ/よ/じゃん), short sentences. Contradiction: sentimental about
  handwriting — keeps every address label they ever re-wrote. *Personal:* a
  letter they chose not to deliver — from a dying father in Saltglass to his
  estranged daughter **Umi** in Lanternfall, asking forgiveness she had no
  obligation to give. Nao judged it unfair to burden her. Resolution (Ch5):
  Nao delivers it and lets Umi decide; the letter's ambiguity is the point.
- **Mio** (ミオ), apothecary. Rolled sleeves, carefully labelled bottles,
  straightens things while thinking. Gentle with frightened people; stubborn
  when kindness would become dishonesty. Polite-ish speech (です/ます to
  strangers, softer plain with friends). Contradiction: a fierce, funny temper
  when someone is condescending. *Personal:* the limits of being useful to
  everyone — she cannot say no. In Lanternfall, where "no" is disappearing,
  she has to refuse something important out loud.
- **Ren** (レン), travelling lantern keeper. Patched ceremonial coat, lamp
  polished far better than their boots. Earnest, precise, unexpectedly funny
  when comfortable. Careful speech, occasionally formal; makes deadpan puns.
  Contradiction: terrible at directions despite the job. *Personal:* their
  mentor **Master Ushio** — Ren remembers every teaching and not the face. The
  Hush took it; Ushio went to the Archive years ago to argue with Kasane and
  never returned. Snowbell's observatory holds a sketch; the Archive holds
  the rest. Ren chooses whether to take the memory back, knowing it ends in
  their last quarrel.
- **Suzu** (スズ), travelling performer. Faded stage ribbon, expressive
  posture, answers hard questions with a joke before an honest answer.
  Playful speech, stage phrases. Contradiction: meticulous about accounts and
  debts. *Personal:* a comforting lie that outlived its purpose — years ago
  she told a little boy in Cinder Orchard, **Hiro**, that his mother had gone
  ahead with the troupe and would come back with spring. She had died in the
  orchard fire. Hiro (now grown, a glassblower) still keeps a seat for her
  each festival. Resolution (Ch3): Suzu tells him the truth.

Unchosen candidates stay in the world with lives of their own (Nao keeps
couriering, Mio runs her shop, Ren tends Reedwake's lanterns, Suzu performs)
and appear in later chapters with small updates. They do not resent the player.

## Chapters
1. **Reedwake** (葦ノ瀬 あしのせ) — riverside village after a storm. Keeper
   Tsuru; Hana's teahouse (two cups of tea); broken bridge to the far bank.
   Quest 1 "Labels After the Storm" (嵐のあとのラベル): labels, letters,
   lantern names and signs went blank in the storm — too cleanly. Quest 2
   "The Mill That Calls Back" (呼び返す水車小屋): the abandoned upstream mill
   echoes voices from years ago; all four candidates respond. Boss: the Mill
   Echo. Restoring the mill lantern's name reconnects the bridge; Hana's
   brother **Kōji** crosses with his own cup. Recruitment in the Lantern Hall;
   departure west.
2. **Saltglass** (潮硝子 しおがらす) — working harbour. Cargo labels,
   letters, contradictory directions; tides and ferry routes. Theme: a
   misunderstanding (the Hush makes labels contradict) vs a deliberate lie
   (clerk **Wataru** re-labels cargo to hide debts). The **Drowned Archive**
   (tidal ruin, accessible at low tide) — a flooded branch of the Still
   Archive; names are being "shelved". Boss: the Tide Clerk (stamps
   "returned to sender" on names). Learn the Archive exists and is active.
3. **Cinder Orchard** (灰実の里 はいみのさと) — terraces, workshops, the
   sealed glass kiln. The official chronicle says the orchard never burned;
   residents half-remember a fire twenty years ago. Practical consequence:
   firebreaks were abandoned "because there has never been a fire". Suzu's
   Hiro lives here. Dungeon: terraced fields → workshops → sealed kiln. Boss:
   the Kiln Warden (heat; water/ice answer it). Village chooses to restore the
   painful memory and rebuild the firebreaks.
4. **Snowbell** (雪鈴 ゆきすず) — mountain hamlet beneath the observatory.
   Weather, isolation, family letters, an old promise: astronomer **Hoshino**
   promised his daughter **Akari** (now a Lanternfall clerk) to keep the
   observatory lamp lit until she comes home; their letters stopped because
   the Hush took the addresses. A quiet snowed-in stretch at the inn where the
   two travellers talk (companion scene). Dungeon: the frozen observatory.
   Star charts show the Archive's location and a sketch of Master Ushio.
5. **Lanternfall** (灯落 ひおち) — beautiful administrative town where
   disagreement is disappearing from speech: people cannot say no, only
   "certainly". Public records, the **submerged bell tower** (flooded thirty
   years ago), and the hidden infrastructure of the Hush (quiet conduits
   carrying names uphill). Learn Kasane's story (the flood, the vague promise,
   Tōya). Ringing the drowned bell restores disagreement. Nao's letter to Umi;
   Mio's refusal; Akari reunites with Hoshino via letter.
6. **The Still Archive** (静寂の書庫) — final connected dungeon. Kasane. The
   climax resolves through action AND interpretation. Choices (not good/bad):
   (a) the grieving who gave memories willingly — return all, or let each
   person choose to visit theirs; (b) the Archive — close it, or re-found it
   as an open library of names; (c) Kasane — bring them down to Lanternfall to
   face those harmed, or leave them to keep the new Archive under the
   companions' watch. Interpretation: understand Tōya's last message — the
   vague promise read in context — and Kasane's own words.
   Final combat is against the Hush itself; after it, a conversation.

## Ending & after
Proper denouement: walk back through changed towns; see the people you
helped. Companion-specific epilogue. Then the **Unwritten Atlas** unlocks:
unstable routes left behind as the world recovers (unmoored names, half-built
roads). The story stays resolved.

## Long roads: two quest lines across the chapters
Optional side quests (src/content/lq/) that start in Reedwake, gain a step in
each later region, and send the player back to earlier places. Neither gates
the main story; the main story only paces their later steps. Both happened in
"the storm year" thirty years ago — the same season that flooded Lanternfall
and washed away Reedwake's bridge (a background link only; neither line
touches Kasane's story).

**A — "A Fare Thirty Years Owed" (三十年の渡し賃, quest `lq_fare`).** Kōji
and Hana's late mother, the ferrywoman, kept a fare book. One fare was never
paid: on a storm night a fifteen-year-old kitchen girl from the Gull in
Saltglass ("Sister Dove" — Chigusa) crossed to reach her dying mother, left the
Gull's wooden seal (lent to her by Tamae's mother so inns would trust her) as a
pledge and wrote "I will come back to pay, without fail". Her mother died; she
drifted inland, worked three winters in Fusa's kitchen in Cinder Orchard, and
now keeps a tea stall at the Snowbell pass, a cup always turned upside down
"for someone I owe". She never went back: first no money, then shame. The
storm of chapter 1 lifted her name from the book; the carved seal kept its
word, かもめ.
1. Ch1 Reedwake — Kōji (teahouse) asks you to look at the book; the ferry
   house: read the entry and stamp the seal (challenge `lq.c_seal`).
2. Ch2 Saltglass — Tamae recognises her mother's seal; remembers Sister Dove,
   her spoon-standing tea and a nameless postcard from the orchards.
3. Ch3 Cinder Orchard — Fusa knew her as Chigusa and her spare cup; she went up
   the snow road. (Attentive players recognise the tea at the stall and can
   skip this step.)
4. Ch4 Snowbell road — Chigusa's stall: her story; the companion's words
   decide her; a note sent ahead (challenge `lq.c_note`). **`lq_ally1`**: the
   companion resolves not to wait to be asked — in battle they now act on
   their own initiative (framed per companion).
5. Back to Reedwake (once the Snowbell road is open, ch4_done) — Chigusa pays
   Kōji at Hana's teahouse; her name goes back into the book, "Paid".
6. Back to Saltglass — she returns the seal to Tamae herself. Reward: the kept
   cup (charm, +1 resolve). Afterwards she is at her stall again.

**B — "The Name Nobody Calls" (誰も呼ばない名前, quest `lq_road`).** Across
Reedwake's river, past Kōji's ferry house, was Koharuno (小春野, fictional), a
hamlet of ten houses around one great persimmon tree. After the flood its
people left downriver; nobody called its name, the lantern went blank and the
road forgot itself (a path that turns you back). The name survived borrowed
by a fruit: families who fled with cuttings grafted them in Cinder Orchard,
where the "Koharu" persimmon is called out loud every picking day.
1. Ch1 Reedwake (after the bridge) — the blank lantern and the looping path
   past the ferry house; a broken marker ends "…野へ". Old Yasu lived there
   twenty years (his wife Mitsu's home); he remembers the tree, not the name.
2. Ch2 Saltglass — Tetsu's father carried the families downriver; some went
   inland with persimmon cuttings. (Tetsu also starts the line himself.)
3. Ch3 Cinder Orchard — Grandma Ume: the Koharu persimmon, grafted from a great
   tree east across a river; she sends a dried one for "the old man".
4. Back to Reedwake — Yasu tastes it and remembers: Koharuno. He waits by the
   lantern; the name is written (challenge `lq.c_koharu`). Koharuno's custom:
   those who crossed together write both names on the shade — the
   companion writes theirs beside yours. **`lq_ally2`**: they will stand with
   you in battle (framed per companion). The road opens.
5. Koharuno (the side area, maps `lq.koharu` and `lq.koharu_hut`) — the tree
   with a child's height marks ("Kayo, 12"), the stone of names, Yasu and
   Mitsu's doorpost, the tree-keeper's ledger ("sending our girl to my sister
   in Lanternfall") and a chest with a kakishibu-dyed cloth (keepsake).
6. Ch5 Lanternfall — Kayo, the tree-keeper's daughter, tends a persimmon grown
   from a seed she carried at twelve. Before the bell she can only say
   "certainly"; after it she can say she is afraid, and decide to go home.
7. Koharuno — Kayo picks the first fruit and calls "Koharu!", cuts a new
   height mark ("Kayo, 42"). Reward: persimmon-seed charm (harmony +1). She
   stays; later lines say families are coming back.

Companions: every step has a line for each of Nao, Mio, Ren and Suzu (Nao:
letters and addresses; Mio: saying no, plants, care; Ren: lantern names and
Master Ushio's sayings, and not his sense of direction; Suzu: debts, accounts,
stage talk). Letters from home (Kōji's, with the seal's stamp; Tsuru's, about
the lantern) reach players who left Reedwake without hearing of a line, in the
next town they enter after chapter 2.

## Style notes for Japanese
- Natural Japanese for speaker and situation; fantasy formality is not a model
  for everyday speech. Tsuru: plain, elderly, dry (〜じゃ is NOT used; she
  speaks plainly with だね/かい). Kasane: soft polite speech, precise.
- Beginner-facing lines can be simple but must stay natural.
- English renderings are faithful, not word-for-word.
